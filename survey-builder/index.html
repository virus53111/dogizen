Start with a question your survey can answer

Before opening an editor, write a single sentence about the decision the survey should inform. For example: Which part of our onboarding confuses new customers? That goal will help you choose useful questions and cut those that do not belong.

For a customer satisfaction survey, try one overall rating question followed by a short open-ended question. For product research, ask about the tasks someone wants to complete rather than asking them to predict what they might buy. Avoid questions that push respondents toward a particular answer.

A practical survey structure

Murdilimax Forms supports text fields, multiple-choice questions, checkboxes, dates and other common inputs. Multi-page forms make longer questionnaires feel manageable, with progress steps instead of one long wall of questions.

  1. Introduce the purpose and approximate scope of the survey in plain language.
  2. Put quick, low-effort questions first so respondents understand the topic.
  3. Group questions about the same subject on one page.
  4. Use conditional logic to show follow-up questions only when relevant.
  5. Finish with an optional comment field and a clear thank-you.

Share the survey and review answers

Publish the survey and send its unique public link by email, chat or another channel your audience already uses. Respondents do not need a Murdilimax account to answer a public form.

Review submissions in your private dashboard and export them as CSV. For numeric questions, look at distributions rather than just averages; for text answers, group common themes without disclosing personal details.

Survey ideas for different teams

Customer experience: ask what went well, what caused friction and whether the person found what they needed. Event feedback: ask about the venue, session usefulness and one improvement for next time. Internal team pulse: ask about current blockers and whether teammates have the information needed to work well.

Keep data collection proportional. Do not promise anonymity if the form collects identifiable details. Test the published survey on a phone to catch confusing labels and choices before sending it widely.
